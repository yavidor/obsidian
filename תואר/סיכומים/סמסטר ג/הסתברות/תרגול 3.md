# Question ${1}$ 
Let $\Omega=\prod_{n\in \mathbb{N}}\left\{ A,B, \ldots,Z \right\}$ and let the letter at each coordinate be independent from one another and distribute according to the same probability vector $\left( p_{A},\ldots,p_{Z} \right)$ such that all probabilities are positive.

> [!NOTE] Definition
> Probability [[וקטור|vector]] $p_{1,\ldots,P_{n}}$ is a vector such that 
> 
> $$
> 	(\forall i\in \left[ n \right] .p_{i}\in \left[ 0,1 \right] )\land \sum_{i=1}^{n} p_{i} = 1
> $$

Let $m\in \mathbb{N}$ and $x \in \left\{ A, \ldots, Z \right\}^{m}$.
Prove that
$$
	p\left( \exists i\in \mathbb{N}.\left( \omega_{i+1},\ldots,\omega_{i+m} \right) =x \right) =1
$$
## Solution ${1}$
We have 
$$
\left\{\omega \in\Omega: \exists i\in \mathbb{N}.\left( \omega_{im+1},\ldots,\omega_{\left( i+1 \right)m} \right)=x \right\} \subseteq \left\{ \omega \in\Omega: \exists i\in \mathbb{N}.\left( \omega _{i+1},\ldots,\omega_{i+m} \right)=x \right\}
$$
Let's call the first set $A$ and the second set $B$, than we have
$$
	A\subseteq B\implies B^{c}\subseteq A^{c}\implies p\left( B^{c} \right) \subseteq p\left( A^{c} \right) 
$$
And so, it is sufficient to prove that $p\left( A^{c} \right)=0$
We define $A_{k}=\left\{ \omega \in\Omega:\forall i\leq k.\left( \omega_{im+1},\ldots,\omega_{\left( i+1 \right)m} \right)\neq x \right\}$, Note that $A^{c}=\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}A_{k}$, additionally. $\forall k\in \mathbb{N}.A_{k+1}\subseteq A_{k}$. Thus by continuity of measure (which we formerly called (I think it's just a name I've given it) [[General Probability Space#Chain of subsets|Chain of Subsets]]), we have
$$
	\mathbb{P}\left( A^{c} \right) =\mathbb{P}\left( \bigcap^{\infty}_{k=1}A_{k} \right) =\lim_{ n \to \infty } \mathbb{P}\left( \bigcap^{n}_{k=1}A_{k} \right) =\lim_{ n \to \infty } \mathbb{P}\left( A_{n} \right) = \lim_{ n \to \infty } \left( 1-\prod_{j=1}^{m} p_{x_{j}} \right)^{n}=0
$$
Where $p_{x_{j}}$ is the value of the coordinate vector at the place for $x_{j}$ (whatever letter that might be)
The reason the limit is $0$ is that it's the limit of $1-\text{smth}$  where $1>\text{smth}>0$ , therefore $1>1-smth> 0$, and raising it to the power of $n$ gives $0$ as $n\to \infty$
