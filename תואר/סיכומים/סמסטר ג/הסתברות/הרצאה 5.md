# Reminder - Random Variables
$\left( \Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P} \right)$ is a [[General Probability Space|Prob. Space]], a [[Random Variables|random variable]] is a function
$$
	X:\Omega\to \mathbb{R}
$$
such taht fo all $a<b$
$$
	\left\{ a<X<b \right\} =\left\{ \omega \in\Omega:a<X\left( \omega \right) <b \right\} \in \mathcal{F}
$$
# Example
Urn with ${20}$ balls numbered $1-20$. Choose ${3}$
$$
	\Omega= \left\{ \left( i,j,k \right) :1\leq j<k\leq 20 \right\} 
$$
With the uniform probability.
And then for example we can have $X\left( i,j,k \right)=k$ or $Y\left( i,j,k \right)= \frac{i+j+k}{3}$ 
and even $Z\left( i,j,k \right)=\cos ^{2}i-e^{ jk }$

> [!INFO] Reminder
> For $A\subseteq \mathbb{R}$
> When we write $\left\{ X\in A \right\}$ (which is nonsense)
> we actually mean $\left\{ \omega \in\Omega:X\left( \omega \right)\in A \right\}$
> Which in normal notation for [[פונקציה|functions]] is written as $X^{-1}\left( A \right)$

We might ask $\mathbb{P}\left( X<11.5 \right)=\mathbb{P}\left( \left\{ \omega \in\Omega:X\left( \omega \right)<11.5 \right\} \right)$
And the answer is
$$
	\mathbb{P}\left( \underbrace{ \left\{ \left( i,j,k \right) \in\Omega: k < 11.5 \right\}  }_{ C } \right)  \underset{ \text{Uniform} }{ = } \frac{\left| C \right| }{\left| \Omega \right| } = \frac{\binom{11}{3} }{\binom{20}{3} }=\text{A number}
$$

> [!NOTE] Remark
> It turns out that for **every** "Reasonable" set $A\subseteq \mathbb{R}$ (Borel)
> $$
> 	\left\{ X\in A \right\} \in \mathcal{F}
> $$
> So $X$ cannot encounter any "FUGLY SLUTS" (It's a [mean girls reference](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CF8f-rqKI1U))

# Example
Consider the discrete space
$$
	\left( \Omega_{0},p \right) 
$$
Where $\Omega_{0}=\left\{ H,T \right\}$
where
$$
	p\left( \omega \right) = \begin{cases}
	 \tilde{p} & \omega=H \\
	 1-\tilde{p} & \omega=T
	\end{cases}
$$
For a number $0\leq \tilde{p}\leq 1$
We ask about $\Omega=\Omega_{0}^{n}= \underbrace{ \Omega_{0}\times\Omega_{0}\times\ldots\times\Omega_{0} }_{ n\text{ times} }$
With the product prob function.
if e.g $n=7$
$$
	\mathbb{P}\left( \left\{ HHTTHTH \right\}  \right) = \tilde{p}\cdot \tilde{p}\cdot \left( 1-\tilde{p} \right) \cdot \left( 1-\tilde{p} \right) \cdot \tilde{p}\cdot \left( 1-\tilde{p} \right)\cdot \tilde{p}=\tilde{p}^{4}\left( 1-\tilde{p} \right) ^{3}
$$
define
$$
	\begin{gather}
	X:\Omega\to \mathbb{R} \\
	\left( \omega_{1},\ldots,\omega _{n} \right) \mapsto \left| \left\{ 1\leq i\leq n:\omega_{i}=H \right\}  \right| =\text{Number of heads}
	\end{gather}
$$
So $X\left( H H T T H T H \right)=4$
Note $\mathbb{P}\left( X \in \left\{ 0,1,2,\ldots,n \right\} \right)=1$ since $X\in \left\{ 0,1,2,\ldots,n \right\}=\Omega$
But we want to ask about a specific $k \in \left[n\right]$.
$$
	\mathbb{P}\left( X=k \right) =\mathbb{P}\left( \underbrace{ \left\{ \left( \omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n} \right) \in\Omega:X\left( \omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n} \right) =k \right\} }_{ A }  \right) 
$$
Since we are in a [[Discrete Probability Space]] (and also because we're running over disjoint events), we have
$$
	\begin{gather}
	\mathbb{P}\left( A \right) =\sum_{\left( \omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n} \right)\in A } \mathbb{P}\left( \left\{ \left( \omega_{1},\ldots,\omega _{n} \right)  \right\}  \right)  \\
	= \sum_{\left( \omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n} \right)\in A } \tilde{p}^{k}\left( 1-\tilde{p} \right) ^{n-k} \\
	= \left| A \right|  \cdot \tilde{p}^{k} \left( 1-\tilde{p} \right) ^{n-k} = \binom{n}{k}\tilde{p}^{k}\left( 1-p \right) ^{n-k}
	\end{gather}
$$
Let's do a fast sanity check to see we didn't do anything stupid
$$
	\begin{gather}
	1=\mathbb{P}\left( \Omega \right) =\mathbb{P}\left( \underbrace{ \bigcup_{k=1}^{n} }_{ \text{disjoint} }\left\{ X=k \right\}  \right)  \\
	= \sum_{k=1}^{n} \mathbb{P}\left( X=k \right) = \sum_{k=1}^{n} \binom{n}{k}\tilde{p}^{k}\left( 1-\tilde{p} \right) ^{n-k}=\left( \tilde{p}+\left( 1-\tilde{p} \right)  \right) ^{n}
	\end{gather}
$$

## Another example
Take the same $\Omega_{0}$ and
$$
	\Omega=\Omega_{0}^{\infty}=\left\{ \left( \omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3},\ldots \right) :\omega_{i}\in \left\{ H,T \right\}  \right\} 
$$
With the [[Product Space|product prob]], this is a non-discrete space
define
$$
	\begin{gather}
	X:\Omega\to \mathbb{R} \\
	\left( \omega_{1},\omega_{2},\ldots \right) \mapsto \min\left\{ i:\omega_{i} = H \right\} 
	\end{gather}
$$
e.g
$X\left( T T T T H HT H T H\ldots  \right)=5$
and still $\mathbb{P}\left( X\in \left\{ 1,2,3,\ldots \right\} \right)=1$
And for $k \in \mathbb{N}$
$$
	\begin{gather}
	
	\mathbb{P}\left( X=k \right) = \mathbb{P}\left( \left\{ \left( \omega_{1},\omega_{2},\ldots \right) :\begin{gathered}\omega_{1}=\omega_{2}=\ldots=\omega_{k-1}=T\\ \omega_{k}=H\end{gathered} \right\}  \right)  \\
	= \mathbb{P}\left( \underbrace{ \left\{ T \right\} \times \left\{ T \right\} \times\ldots\times \left\{ T \right\} }_{ k-1 } \times \left\{ H \right\} \times \Omega_{0}^{\infty}  \right)  \\
	= \left( 1-\tilde{p} \right) ^{k-1} \cdot \tilde{p}\cdot1= \left( 1-\tilde{p} \right) ^{k-1}\cdot \tilde{p} 
	\end{gather}
$$
And again
$$
	\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}  \left( 1-\tilde{p} \right) ^{k-1}\tilde{p}=1
$$
### Remark
Foramlly $X$ is not defined on $(T, T, T, T, T, \ldots)$
but since
$$
	\mathbb{P}\left( \left( T,T,T,T,T,T,\ldots \right)  \right) =0
$$
We may define arbitrarily whatever we want, like $X\left( \left( T,T,T,T,\ldots \right) \right)=24601$

# Example
The experiment is flipping a <u>fair</u> coin ${9}$ times
$$
	\begin{gather}
	X=\text{\# of heads} \\
	X=\text{\# of tails} 
	\end{gather}
$$
We saw that $\mathbb{P}\left( X\in \left\{ 0,1,\ldots,9 \right\} \right)=1$ and 
$$
\mathbb{P}\left( X=k \right)=\binom{n}{k}\tilde{p}^{k}\left( 1-\tilde{p} \right)^{n-k}\underset{ \tilde{p}=\frac{1}{2} }{ = } \binom{9}{k}\left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^{9}
$$
and
$$
	\mathbb{P}\left( Y=k \right) = \mathbb{P}\left( X=9-k \right) =\binom{9}{9-k} \left( \frac{1}{2} \right) ^{9}= \binom{9}{k}\left( \frac{1}{2} \right) ^{9}
$$
So $\mathbb{P}_{X}=\mathbb{P}_{Y}$ even though $X\neq Y$ as functions.
